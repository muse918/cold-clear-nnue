# TETR.IO placement-level battle rules audit

## Authority and version

The authority for this port is the **user-supplied TETR.IO Beta 1.7.8 client, engine 19**, client commit `e84ebccd2`:

```

It extracts the original attack-manager and clear-manager factories into a Node VM. The fixture generator does not substitute a second handwritten attack formula as its reference.

## Scope

The environment represents a 10-column, 20-visible-row board with 20 buffer rows. It accepts reachable placement actions generated by this engine, resolves lock, whole-row deletion, attack, cancellation, ready garbage, hold/preview consumption and next spawn. It supports standard tetrominoes and the exposed room-rule options, including all-spin variants.

The user explicitly excluded input/gravity simulation. This port therefore has no DAS/ARR, gravity, lock delay, animation timing or network connection. It also omits unrelated Zenith progression, actors, bomb tiles, multiplayer targeting transport and scoring/visual statistics. Optional target-bonus attack arithmetic is present, but it does not implement a multiplayer networking client.

The code supports **instant garbage entry** with externally supplied packet readiness. Custom continuous/delayed entry, packet sleeping/caution countdowns, garbage-favor weighting, nonstandard boards/minos and Zenith modifiers are outside this implementation. They must not be silently mapped to the supported model.

## Lock and attack ordering

`State.combo` and `State.b2b` store the official counters, initially zero. The first ordinary clear changes combo to one. A first difficult clear changes B2B to one. A no-clear resets combo but preserves B2B.

For every lock:

1. Advance the explicitly supplied frame interval; add the piece; identify full rows and garbage provenance.
2. Delete complete rows, preserving the order of survivors. Empty rows enter at the top. Permanent material prevents an ordinary row clear and is ignored for PC emptiness.
3. Increment the placed-piece counter before cancellation. Count the clear and add its B2B increments.
4. If a non-difficult clear breaks charged B2B, emit the three surge slices **before** the main attack.
5. Apply the base clear/spin attack, B2B bonus, combo formula, multiplier/rounding, then the one-line downstack bonus and attack cap.
6. Resolve the main attack against incoming packets, then resolve the separate PC packet.
7. If combo blocking did not block this lock, tank ready packets up to the cap.
8. Finish hold/preview consumption, apply lockout policy where enabled, and check the next piece's actual spawn.

### Attack details that affect search

| Lines | Ordinary | Mini spin | Full spin |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 1 | 0 | 0 | 2 |
| 2 | 1 | 1 | 4 |
| 3 | 2 | 2 | 6 |
| 4 | 4 | 4 | 10 |

A mini quad therefore has base attack 4 in the official source. Triangle's generic quad-spin branch produces 10, so that branch was not copied.

For League, consecutive difficult clears add the flat B2B bonus 1 while charge accumulates. Charging does not remove that flat bonus. With official combo counter `c`, the multiplier is `1 + 0.25*(c-1)` for `c>1`; for `c>2`, the attack is at least `log1p(1.25*(c-1))` before garbage multiplier and rounding. Classic/modern guideline tables and B2B chaining are separately selectable options.

By default a PC quad or spin receives **two** B2B increments: the difficult clear and the PC increment. A PC ordinary single receives the PC increment only. `allclear_b2b_dupes=false`, `allclear_b2b_sends` and `allclear_charges` change this behavior explicitly. The official suppression of the B2B attack bonus when the PC increment is the only increment is retained.

When B2B `b` breaks above threshold `t`, surge size is `floor((b-t+base)*multiplier)`. Its first two slices are `round(size/3)` and the third is the remainder. Zero slices are skipped. Packet boundaries affect opener cancellation and garbage RNG, so they cannot be replaced with one summed packet.

Downstack special bonus is one line when a quad or any spin clears a row with garbage provenance. It is applied **after** multiplier/rounding and **before** the attack cap. The cap does not cap the separate PC or surge packets.

## Cancellation, packet order and RNG

Official cancellation considers inactive/unconfirmed packets and packets that are not yet spawn-ready. It skips hardened packets. The base attack is consumed first, then additional cancellation; extra cancellation never becomes outgoing garbage.

Opener extra cancellation is enabled when `pieces_placed <= opener_phase` and the unshielded pending amount is at least total garbage already sent. It is evaluated for each packet, including every surge slice and the separate PC packet. `total_sent` is actual outgoing garbage after cancellation.

Tanking selects packets with `active && spawn_ready`. A waiting packet at the front does not block a ready packet behind it. The official routine temporarily removes waiting packets and appends them after the remaining ready packets, which changes future cancellation order. This order is preserved.

The Park–Miller RNG uses modulus 2147483647 and multiplier 16807. A comparison still consumes RNG when its probability is zero or one. Packet completion, including cancellation to zero, performs the messiness-change comparison and possibly a hole reroll. An explicit hole avoids the inner-hole RNG draw, but packet completion still consumes its draws. The full RNG seed, last hole and hole-change flag therefore belong in state and cache keys.

Hardened packet metadata means cancellation protection. It does **not** turn ordinary garbage cells into permanent/solid cells.

### Receiving and absolute cap

`enqueue_garbage` applies receive multiplier/rounding, then the absolute cap. A packet that crosses the cap is truncated. If the queue is already at the cap, the official client retains the new packet as **shielded** until its confirmation/hit handling removes it. The core preserves that distinction; the adapter owns the corresponding confirmation event. Absolute-cap counting includes shielded queued amounts, while the pending count used by opener cancellation excludes shielded packets.

## Board overflow, PC and spawn

The official garbage-smash guard checks whether the **entire top buffer row is occupied**. It does not reject every partially occupied row that is shifted out of the buffer. The board helper reproduces that behavior. For ordinary League garbage the new row is inserted at the bottom. Where permanent material is supplied, the official insertion location above the highest permanent tile is retained, including the negative-index splice edge case when such material occupies a partial top row.

Garbage hole width expands to the right of the chosen column and then to the left at the right board edge. For example, column 9 with width 3 leaves columns 7, 8 and 9 empty.

Height above row 20 alone is not a topout condition. The environment checks the actual next spawn and clutch rule. When lockout is enabled, an entirely above-ceiling lock is fatal unless it clears and clutch is enabled; the clear and attack still happen before that lockout decision. League disables that lockout rule.

An empty observed preview queue is an unknown search horizon. It does not mean the player died or authorize inventing the next piece. An empty hold consumes one preview for the placed piece, and a further preview for the new current piece. An occupied hold consumes only the new current piece from the preview queue.

## Integration boundary: required snapshot data

This core is intentionally independent of the bot account and network connection. A real adapter must supply:

- Board occupancy and ordinary/permanent garbage provenance in the documented bottom-first coordinates.
- Current piece, hold, hold-used flag and observed preview queue excluding current.
- Official combo/B2B counters, previous-clear flag, pieces placed and actual total sent.
- Already-reconciled incoming packets, their order, confirmation/readiness, hole/width/protection metadata and identifiers.
- Current garbage RNG state, last hole/change flag, frame and last-tank frame.
- Current mutable garbage multiplier and garbage cap for a late-game snapshot.

The League `passthrough=zero` acknowledgement calculation precedes `AddPendingGarbage`: attacks can offset unacknowledged outgoing attacks using their `ackiid`. **Raw network interaction messages must not be passed directly as the reconciled queue.** That acknowledgement history is outside this placement state because it belongs to the transport adapter. The adapter must update shielded packet confirmation/removal and readiness explicitly.

Triangle's garbage implementation contains an acknowledged approximation around active/hit timing. Packet age plus `garbagespeed` alone does not establish official `active && status==spawn`. This implementation accepts the resulting readiness explicitly and does not claim to validate Triangle's transport timing.

Triangle initially uses `-1` for its combo/B2B counters, whereas the official counters start at zero. Adding one is a representation conversion for matching states; it does not correct divergent PC/B2B transition logic. Importing a Triangle B2B counter without checking those earlier transitions is not authoritative.

The official client mutates multiplier/cap by repeated floating additions each frame after the strict `frame > margin` boundary. `advance_frames` preserves those additions. A state with sentinel dynamic values initializes them from the supplied rules; it does not reconstruct an unknown past. Therefore a late-game live snapshot must pass the current values from the client engine. Default `elapsed_frames=0` means search holds time constant unless its caller supplies an estimate; it is not a claim that piece placement takes zero real time.

`state_key` serializes all fields affecting future transitions, including board provenance, queue/hold, counters, incoming order/metadata, RNG, frames and mutable rates. Cache sharing across different rule configurations must additionally isolate the rules; search does so per invocation.

## Validation performed

`battle_parity.json` records **16,592 cases, zero mismatches** against the supplied official client:

- 4,080 deterministic clear/spin/combo/B2B/PC/provenance combinations.
- 12,000 deterministic-seeded randomized battle states spanning the supported attack options, blocking, cancellation, packet readiness, protection, messiness and rounding.
- 512 targeted receive-multiplier and absolute-cap cases, including shielded packets.

The comparison includes 12,040 outgoing packets, 12,493 tank rows and 47,003 cancelled lines. It compares all resulting counters, packet boundaries, pending order and remaining amounts, hole state and RNG seed. These are battle-function checks; they are not evidence of a played online match.

`battle_tests.cpp` additionally passed 80 focused checks covering placement-to-battle composition, whole-row PC, hold/queue use, observed horizon, actual spawn topout, lockout/clutch, garbage top guards, timed multiplier boundaries and numeric limits. All 80 also passed with `-fsanitize=undefined,float-cast-overflow -fno-sanitize-recover=all`. Geometry and search have separate test suites.

The native counters and per-transition line totals are signed 32-bit integers. Arithmetic checks reject combinations whose intermediate or aggregated line counts exceed that representation, rather than converting an out-of-range floating surge or overflowing a signed sum. This includes configurations whose individual values fit the protocol bounds but whose product does not. A rejected `apply_action` returns the unchanged parent state and no partial battle output. Surge remains floored even when the room uses random rounding; range checks consume no extra RNG. Wide arithmetic that still produces a representable result remains supported.

Reproduce with the repository Makefile and the external client:

```sh
make -C tetrio-bot build/battle_probe build/battle_tests
tetrio-bot/build/battle_tests
node tetrio-bot/tests/rules/verify_battle.mjs --official /path/to/tetrio.js
```

Source locators for audit/re-extraction are the pinned `const z=` attack factory, `const K=` clear factory, garbage constants, scalar option defaults and the literal `tetra league` preset. The runtime extractor fails if its file hash differs. This prevents silently treating results from a changed upstream game as the same environment.
